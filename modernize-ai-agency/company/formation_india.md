## 1. Recommended entity: Private Limited Company

### 1.1 Recommendation

Incorporate a **Private Limited Company** that qualifies as a **"small company"** under the Companies Act, 2013:

- Incorporate through **SPICe+**.
- Set authorised capital at **Rs 15 lakh or less**. Rs 1–10 lakh is typical. At this level the MCA filing fee is zero and only state stamp duty is payable.
- The CEO holds all or nearly all of the shares and is a director, designated **Managing Director / CEO**.
- A second trusted individual becomes the second director and second shareholder.

Sources: [MCA SPICe+ FAQ](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/help-faq/faqs/company-services/incorporation/spiceplus-linked-filings.html); [Companies Act 2013](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2013/companies-act%2C-2013.pdf); [s.149](https://indiacode.ecourtsindia.com/companies-act/section/149/).

### 1.2 Why a Private Limited Company

1. **Limited liability is essential.** One USD 50,000 pilot with a "100% behavioural-parity guarantee" is a large contractual exposure. A sole proprietor carries **unlimited** personal liability, which rules that form out.
2. **Foreign buyers expect it.** US, UK, Canadian, Australian and Singaporean procurement teams expect a "Pvt Ltd" counterparty. A Pvt Ltd can also add a co-founder, angel or VC money (including FDI), ESOPs and preference shares without changing its legal form.
3. **The extra compliance over an OPC is small.** Every company needs a statutory audit anyway. A small company gets the following relief:
   - only one board meeting in each half of the calendar year, at least 90 days apart (s.173(5));
   - the abridged **MGT-7A** annual return;
   - **half penalties** under s.446B, capped at Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer.

   From **1 December 2025** the small-company ceiling is **paid-up capital up to Rs 10 crore and turnover up to Rs 100 crore** ([G.S.R. 880(E)](https://gazettetracker.com/g/CG-DL-E-01122025-268124); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2204766)). In practice, the only extra items compared with an OPC are an AGM and a second director.
4. **Startup benefits are open to it.** The company is eligible for DPIIT Startup recognition: within 10 years of incorporation, with turnover never above Rs 200 crore ([G.S.R. 108(E), 4 Feb 2026](https://gazettetracker.com/g/CG-DL-E-05022026-269843)). Only a Pvt Ltd or an LLP can apply for the s.80-IAC tax holiday.
5. **An LLP has no advantage here.** It still needs 2 designated partners, it cannot issue shares, and venture investors generally avoid it. It needs an audit above Rs 40 lakh turnover, which one pilot invoice already exceeds.

### 1.3 Fallback: One Person Company (OPC)

Use an OPC if you have no trusted second person willing to be a director and shareholder.

- **Members and directors:** the CEO is the sole member and director. A nominee who is an adult Indian citizen is also required; the nominee signs consent with a DSC inside SPICe+ Part B.
- **Converting later:** convert to a Pvt Ltd by filing **INC-6 within 30 days of the special resolution** once a co-founder or investor arrives. Since **1 April 2021**, conversion is allowed **at any time**. There are no capital or turnover triggers and no 2-year lock-in ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1694828); [MCA OPC FAQ](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/help-faq/faqs/company-services/incorporation/one-person-company.html)). Some practitioner websites still describe the old Rs 50 lakh / Rs 2 crore conversion rule; it no longer applies.
- **Trade-offs:**
  - No second shareholder, investor or ESOP holder is possible until the company converts.
  - The "(OPC) Private Limited" suffix is less familiar to foreign buyers.
  - Converting means updating client contracts and bank records.
- **Annual compliance differences from a Pvt Ltd:**
  - No AGM is needed (s.96).
  - AOC-4 is due within 180 days of FY close (about 27 September).
  - A single-director OPC is exempt from the board-meeting rules (ss.173–174).

### 1.4 Options considered and rejected

| Form | Why rejected for this business |
|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Unlimited personal liability under the parity guarantee. Many foreign enterprises will not contract with one. Not eligible for DPIIT recognition. |
| LLP | Needs 2 partners. Cannot issue equity or ESOPs. Audit is required anyway once turnover passes Rs 40 lakh, which a single pilot does. Less familiar to US procurement. |
| OPC | Acceptable fallback only (§1.3). |

### 1.5 Duties of the second director (discuss with your CS before filing)

- The second director carries **real statutory duties and liability**. Choose someone you trust and who understands this.
- If the second shareholder holds shares **on the CEO's behalf**, beneficial-interest declarations under **s.89** are needed.
- Record founder vesting and control in a **shareholders' agreement**.
- At least one director must be resident in India for 182 days or more in the FY (s.149(3)). The CEO lives in India, which satisfies this.

## 6. Caveats

- Stated as at 13 October 2026. The **Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026** received its JPC report on 3 August 2026 but has not passed ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-corporate-laws-amendment-bill-2026)). It would raise the small-company limits to Rs 20 crore / Rs 200 crore, change auditor exemptions and allow AGMs by video conference. Recheck its status before filing.
- **DIR-3 KYC** now follows a 3-year cycle from 31 March 2026 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2210552)). When the first KYC falls due for a DIN allotted after 31 March 2026 is unconfirmed; confirm with your CS.
- Stamp duty, DSC prices, professional fees and timelines are practitioner estimates.
- The **31 March 2030** incorporation cut-off for s.80-IAC was not verified against a primary source; confirm with your CA.
- Limited liability does not remove contractual risk. The parity guarantee must carry caps and a sole remedy (legal_operating_policy.md §8).
